# Credits

The `gs` plugin was written by Gradiential Systems. Its skills are written in our own words for people who are not programmers, and its scripts are original. Many of its ideas come from published work, credited here. Where a skill is a close adaptation of a source, that source's licence notice is reproduced below.

## Sources and what was adapted

- **Matt Pocock, `mattpocock/skills`** (MIT). The interview before the spec, the spec written by synthesis, thin end-to-end phases, the test that fails first, the loop-before-theory approach to defects, the two reviewers kept apart, the plain-language re-explanation, the throwaway prototype, the glossary discipline, the retrospective that becomes a check, and the handoff document. The earlier Gradiential toolkit that this plugin grew from was an adaptation of these skills.
- **Addy Osmani, `addyosmani/agent-skills`** (MIT). The gated spec-then-plan flow, the rule against weakening a failing check, the failing test that comes before a fix, the one-question interview with a proposed answer, the evidence rule ("not measured" is an answer), and scope discipline.
- **Jesse Vincent, `obra/superpowers`** (MIT). The hard gate before building and the rule that evidence comes before any claim of completion.
- **GitHub, `github/spec-kit`** (MIT). The cap on clarifying questions asked one at a time, the forced sections of a spec, and the idea of a checklist that tests the requirements themselves.
- **Anthropic, `anthropics/claude-plugins-official` and `anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins`** (Apache-2.0). From the code-modernization plugin: the single front door, the recorded state, the status that ends on one exact next command, the verdict computed by a script, and the deliberate break that proves a check can fail. From champion-kit and the small-business plugin: how to ask one question with a default and a recommendation. From skill-creator: comparing a result with and without a skill.
- **OpenAI, `openai/openai-cookbook`** (MIT). The living plan with progress, surprises and a decision log (the ExecPlan pattern).
- **Fission-AI, `OpenSpec`** (MIT). A change as its own small file, and a review order that lets the reader stop early.
- **BMad Code, `BMAD-METHOD`** (MIT). Two-sentence summaries at each hand-over.
- **Google, `gemini-cli-extensions/conductor`** (Apache-2.0). Progress kept as checkboxes in the plan file.
